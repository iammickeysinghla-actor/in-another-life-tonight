The Film

Over one dinner, a man's carefree night out with a friend collides with the ex he never got to say goodbye to, pulling him back into the night that started it all.

Milo is out to dinner with his friend Ray, sharing plates and toasting to a weekend with no filters. Then Lex walks in, hand in hand with someone new.

Lex is the man Milo was only beginning to fall for, until a fear, a possible move, and a goodbye that never came ended it. Milo retreats, but the restaurant where they had their first date won't let him go. The light, the music, and the memory of that night begin to blur into the present.

A man who has been running from what he feels comes face to face with everything they never said.

Director's Statement

I wrote this film about a goodbye that never happened. Milo and Lex only had the start of something, and that's what makes it so hard to let go of. There's no big fight or clean breakup. Just everything nobody said.

I wanted it to feel like the way you actually remember things. You walk into a place and suddenly you're somewhere else. One song, one look, and you're back in that night. I'm usually more interested in how a moment feels than in what happens in it.

This is my first time directing, so I kept it simple. One restaurant, a few people, and a night that never really ended for one of them.

mickey singh, writer / director

About the director

Mickey Singh is a writer, director, and actor based in Los Angeles. He grew up in India and moved to the U.S. in 2015. He's drawn to stories that are more about a feeling than a big plot, the kind that stay with you after the credits. In Another Life, Tonight is his first time directing. His next short, We Live in LA, is in pre-production.
