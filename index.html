The Film

Over one dinner, a man's carefree night out with a friend collides with the ex he never got to say goodbye to, pulling him back into the night that started it all.

Milo is out to dinner with his friend Ray, toasting to a weekend with no filters. Then Lex walks in, holding hands with someone new.

Milo and Lex were just starting something, and it ended without a goodbye. This is the same restaurant where Milo brought him on the night that mattered most. As the night goes on, the past starts to mix with the present.

Director's Statement

I started thinking about this story while I was packing up to leave New York for LA, looking back on the year I'd spent there. What stayed with me most was the people I met, and how some of those connections ended before they really got going.

That's where Milo and Lex came from. They were just starting something, and then it stopped, and neither of them ever said why. There's no big breakup, so there's no one to blame. Lex isn't cruel, he's scared. Milo isn't a victim, he put real care into that connection.

I wanted the film to feel the way you actually remember things. When you look back on something, you hold on to the good moments, so that's what I focused on. And it doesn't take much to bring them back. A place, a song, and you're right back in that night.

This is my first time directing, so I kept it small. One restaurant, a few people, one night.

mickey singh, writer / director

About the director

Mickey Singh is a writer, director, and actor based in Los Angeles. He's drawn to stories that are more about a feeling than a big plot, the kind that stay with you after the credits. In Another Life, Tonight is his first time directing. His next short, We Live in LA, is in pre-production.
